The Bull Flag pattern: 4 rules, live examples and a real backtest

A Bull Flag is a sharp rally (the pole) followed by a short, orderly pause that drifts slightly down (the flag). When price closes above the top of the flag on strong volume, the pattern has broken out. The 4 rules

1
Pole

A sharp rally of 20% or more in 3 to 8 weeks (15–40 sessions).

2
Flag

5–15 sessions that drift down, giving back less than half of the pole.

3
Volume

Volume dries up inside the flag — the second half quieter than the first.

4
Breakout

A close above the flag's high on at least 1.5× the flag's average volume.

The scanner also requires the stock to be above its 50 and 200-day averages, among the strongest 30% of stocks (relative strength ≥ 70), with a market cap of ₹500 cr+ and ₹5 cr+ median daily turnover.

FAQ

What is a Bull Flag pattern?

A continuation pattern: a sharp rally (the pole) followed by a short, orderly pullback that drifts slightly down (the flag). A close above the top of the flag on strong volume is the breakout.

How reliable is the Bull Flag?

In our backtest of Indian stocks since 2020, about 1 in 3 breakouts worked. It was profitable overall because winners ran much further than losers fell.

Bull Flag vs Pennant — what's the difference?

A flag's pause is a small channel drifting down with roughly parallel lines; a pennant's pause is a small triangle whose highs fall and lows rise until they meet.

How long should a Bull Flag last?

Our scanner looks for flags of 5–15 sessions after a 20%+ pole over 3–8 weeks. Much longer pauses are usually bases, not flags.
