The High Tight Flag pattern: rules, live examples and a real backtest

A High Tight Flag is one of the rarest and strongest continuation patterns: a near-vertical rally of about 90% or more in a few weeks, then a short, tight pause that gives back little, then a breakout. Below are the exact rules our scanner uses, the High Tight Flag setups it found in Indian stocks recently, and how the pattern actually performed since 2020.

The 4 rules

1
Pole

A gain of 90% or more from the pole's low to its top, in at most about 8 weeks.

2
Tight flag

8–25 sessions starting at the pole top, giving back no more than 25%.

3
Volume

Average flag volume at most 0.8× the pole's average volume.

4
Breakout

A close 0.5–8% above the pole top on 1.5× the 50-day volume, above the 50-day average.

Also required: relative strength ≥ 70, market cap ≥ ₹500 cr and ≥ ₹5 cr turnover.

The part nobody tells you

Most breakouts of any pattern fail. A pattern can still work overall when the winners run much further than the losers fall — which is why every trade needs a stop decided before entry, and why one trade proves nothing.

FAQ

What is a High Tight Flag?

A rare continuation pattern: a near-vertical gain of roughly 90–100%+ in up to ~8 weeks, followed by a tight flag that gives back 25% or less, then a breakout above the pole top.

Why is it so rare?

Few stocks double in two months and then hold almost all of the gain. Our scanner finds only a handful a year in Indian stocks.

Is a High Tight Flag risky?

Yes — the stock has already moved a lot, and pullbacks can be sharp. Position size and a fixed stop matter even more here.
