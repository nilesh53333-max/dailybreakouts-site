The Cup and Handle pattern: rules, live examples and a real backtest

A Cup and Handle is a long, rounded U-shaped base (the cup) that recovers to its old high, then a short, shallow dip (the handle). A close above the cup's rim is the breakout. Below are the exact rules our scanner uses, the Cup and Handle setups it found in Indian stocks recently, and how the pattern actually performed since 2020.

The 4 rules

1
Prior uptrend

Up 30% or more in the year before the cup starts — it's a continuation pattern.

2
Cup

12–33% deep, lasting 7–65 weeks, U-shaped (both sides take ≥ 25% of the time — not a V).

3
Handle

1–4 weeks starting near the old high, giving back no more than a third of the cup.

4
Breakout

A close above the right rim (the handle's high) while above the 200-day average.

Also required: relative strength ≥ 70, market cap ≥ ₹500 cr and ≥ ₹5 cr median daily turnover.

The part nobody tells you

Most breakouts of any pattern fail. A pattern can still work overall when the winners run much further than the losers fall — which is why every trade needs a stop decided before entry, and why one trade proves nothing.

FAQ

What is a Cup and Handle pattern?

A long, rounded U-shaped base that recovers to its old high (the cup), followed by a short shallow dip (the handle). A close above the rim is the breakout.

Why must the cup be U-shaped, not V-shaped?

A rounded bottom shows sellers being absorbed slowly over weeks. A V-shaped crash-and-rebound has not shaken out weak holders and fails more often.

How deep should the handle be?

Our scanner allows the handle to give back at most one third of the cup's depth, lasting 1–4 weeks.
